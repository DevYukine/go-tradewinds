# Architecture

## Boundaries

| Package | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `cmd/tradewinds` | Explicit registration, account listing, Fx lifecycle, HTTP listener, worker startup and shutdown |
| `cmd/tw-sim` | Account-scoped backfill, read-only accounting/model validation and offline variant table/JSON; no game client |
| `internal/config` | Environment loading and validated operating limits |
| `internal/gameapi` | Current typed API contract, HTTP/SSE transport, scoped rate limits, retries and raw observations |
| `internal/store` | PostgreSQL migrations, encrypted credentials/journal, missions, snapshots, diagnostics, settlements and metrics |
| `internal/engine` | Economic decisions, durable mission execution, authoritative reconciliation and eligible reinvestment |
| `internal/sim` | Temporal replay reader, shared-depth markets, fixed fleet/accounting, validation, calibration and production-planner policy loops |
| `internal/httpapi` | Bounded read-only views of database state, readiness and metrics |
| `internal/names` | Vendored canonical Pokemon catalog and unused-name selection |
| `web` | Next.js dashboard; no direct game credentials or game requests |

The Go process runs independently of the dashboard. Closing a browser does not stop trading. Opening more tabs does not create more upstream sessions. PostgreSQL is the durable operational record, not an optional cache.

The simulator reuses the engine's pure state/input builders and planners, not its Runner or executor. Each variant owns its residual books, ships, vaults, contracts and sourcing ledger. A monotone tape cursor restricts observation time and phase; recorded factors bound private port intelligence. Validation-only current ledger evidence never enters policy state. `OpenReadOnly` skips migrations and enforces read-only PostgreSQL sessions for validation and comparisons. Simulated settlements and results are CLI-only and are never written to database tables or served to the dashboard. Live receipt stress defaults off and nil sourcing cash share retains 200 bps.

## Account isolation

Each enabled account has its own credentials, company identity, mission namespace, event cursor, and executor. Credentials and action payloads are encrypted with AES-GCM under a local 32-byte master key; associated data binds ciphertext to its record identity. Dashboard data never includes decrypted credentials or raw action journals.

A pinned PostgreSQL advisory-lock connection protects account execution. Loss of ownership cancels the runner's context rather than transparently acquiring a new lock. Cancellation cannot undo an upstream request already sent, so a takeover must resolve pending journal entries before creating another dependent action. Company uniqueness prevents importing the same company under two independently executable local identities.

Monitor checks, write guards and cleanup serialize access to that pinned connection through the complete result-reader lifetime. A `database/sql` call returning does not mean pgx has released its active rows. This prevents false ownership loss from overlapping checks without suppressing real lock or connection failures.

## Mutation lifecycle

1. Refresh the relevant authoritative data.
2. Persist the mission and its immutable action identity/payload.
3. Send with that action's idempotency key.
4. Record the result durably.
5. Refresh cargo, stores, funds, contracts, or voyage state affected by the result.
6. Advance the mission from actual outcomes, including partial fills.

Network ambiguity is not failure evidence. A timeout or upstream 5xx retains the original intent. A successful response whose body cannot be interpreted is committed-but-uninterpreted, not permission to try again with another key. An in-flight 409 also remains unresolved. A definitive rejection may lead to a new, separately recorded plan after fresh state and any cooldown.

The upstream replay window is 24 real hours. Stop unresolved retries before that window expires, with a margin for in-flight request duration. Authoritative reconciliation must establish an outcome before a replacement action is allowed. Retaining the old key forever does not make it safe to replay forever.

Conflict recovery distinguishes definitive rejection from an ambiguous in-flight request. Freight tender refusals persist a bounded retry deadline; fresh active-contract and full-manifest evidence permit a new tender identity after that deadline. Fresh terminal contract states can settle the obligation without waiting for another tender. Charter refusals retain the observed offer terms and reopen when authoritative terms change. Response prose cannot prove a default, payment or delivery.

An attributed unresolved ship action quarantines dependent spending, not unrelated safe delivery credits. An orphaned pending request whose resources cannot be identified still blocks execution, except for zero-cost bulletin posts. The `__bulletin` control mission runs only after trading is idle; its unresolved requests hold further posts, never trading. Reconciliation and replay always use the persisted request's contract, ship and payload, never a replacement plan's identity. Short sealed manifests are held, not replenished with ordinary cargo or repeatedly tendered as the originally accepted amount.

Registration follows the same model. The generated passphrase is stored before registration; player creation, key creation, company incorporation, and company-key creation have stable independent action identities. A handle conflict cannot cause an automatic fallback name. Company creation is reconciled against the player's actual company list.

### Ship identity and exits

The [0.7.3 fleet schema](https://tradewinds.kibb.io/api-docs/openapi.json) carries nullable `ship.fate` and sibling `former_names [{name, renamed_at}]` on both fleet lists and ship detail. Typed game API models, normalized engine state, retained snapshots and dashboard payloads preserve them. `renamed_at` is when the old name was dropped; history stays with the hull through a sale and is newest first. Dashboard statuses show the fate rather than treating every gone hull as a sinking.

All `status: sunk` hulls are inactive. Only `lost_at_sea`, `captured` and legacy missing-fate records count as voyage hull losses; `seized_smuggling`, `seized_bailiff`, `seized_bankruptcy`, `broken_up` and `royal_navy` are non-voyage exits. Known non-voyage fates correct retained inferred hull losses and do not supply voyage cargo-shortfall evidence. Actual cash accounting, quote-owned hazard estimates and authoritative insurance-policy claims are unchanged; no fate invents a claim payout or a financial loss streak.

`internal/names` recognizes canonical Pokemon names and the numbered generations it emits. Allocation includes current and sunk names, every former name, persisted build/rename intents and the permanent `_used_ship_names` set, saving the reservation before staging any mutation. An invalidated stream-control or financial capture defers allocation until resynchronization, retaining the caller's authority hold without burning a name. The optional `__rename` control gives an owned afloat non-catalog-named hull its next unused catalog name with staged `POST /v1/fleet/rename-ship`. It never replaces an ordinary ship mission, renames an already catalog-named hull or touches an open sale listing. First-send preflight rechecks ownership, name, afloat state and fresh listings. A domain 4xx refusal, excluding authentication and rate limits, deletes the control after persisting a refusal latch keyed by hull ID and current name/status; that unchanged hull is skipped while other eligible hulls proceed, and its allocated name remains used. A name/status change clears the latch. Rate limits retain the intent with a five-real-minute minimum backoff and honor a longer `Retry-After`. Ambiguity retains the original request and key; REST can establish the matching hull/name or dated former-name outcome, or reconcile the action as moot when a fresh fleet capture proves the hull is gone or no longer owned. A completed control waits for that fleet evidence before releasing so an older capture cannot stage a duplicate rename. Attached zero-cost rename and bulletin requests do not block unrelated growth, financing or loan prepayment while their outcomes remain unresolved; an orphaned rename still requires its original mission to be restored before other mutations.

Preparing or resolving a rename does not end a fleet planning pass: that pass can still stage ordinary trading or growth. The rename transport retains its durable journal and original-key recovery.

## Events and freshness

SSE is an invalidation channel. REST and settled action responses determine monetary state. Event IDs are opaque strings, not numeric sequence counters. Persist event deduplication and cursor advancement atomically. Reconnect with the recorded cursor; a replay gap requires cursor reset and authoritative resynchronization, not a loop using the same rejected cursor.

Publish a paginated dataset only after all pages succeed. Retain the last good value on refresh failure and publish an explicit per-dataset error/freshness marker. Refreshing the clock must not make stale cash or cargo appear current. Economic writes require fresh, coherent inputs.

The `loans` dataset paginates borrower-owned active and defaulted loans; any open loan action, player request or financing intent also requires closed-loan history for reconciliation. Every loan capture wave refreshes validated `loan_requests` from `/v1/loan/requests?mine=true&limit=50`; ship-lien admission also needs complete own ship listings. Publish only complete validated authority. Failed captures retain last-good debt reserves and block new borrowing, not reserve-safe stores, sales, deliveries or docking. Debt reserves join regional requirements before discretionary allocation; [Capacity financing](strategy.md#capacity-financing) is normative.

The low-frequency authenticated R&D datasets `rnd_status` and `rnd_register` read only `/v1/rnd/status` and `/v1/rnd/register`, at most once per account per 30 real minutes, including after failures. They retain last-good raw responses and dataset freshness/error metadata; owned patents, licences and component inventories are not fetched. The register is evidence for later rival analysis, not investment authority. The `rnd` decision records eligibility and estimated cost without authorizing research, patent, licence, manufacturing or fitting investment mutations. Identical details deduplicate, but changing player net worth normally produces about one decision per 30-minute poll.

R&D diagnostics currently use the generic two-minute stale display, not a dataset-specific expected-cadence mechanism. No such mechanism has been identified. Between scheduled 30-minute polls, a stale display without an error is expected and does not by itself indicate a failed fetch.

Each refresh runs independent GET jobs through at most six workers on the account's existing rate-limited client. Dependent waves still wait for their prerequisites; pagination and the explicit contract-status reads remain ordered within their job. Workers return private results and request timing. Only the runner reduces them into the snapshot, in deterministic order, after the wave has joined. Cancellation joins all workers and publishes no partial generation. Mutations remain serialized and do not overlap capture workers.

An actual CLI fixture capture completed 31 GETs in 0.519 real seconds; those requests' measured service times summed to 1.255 seconds. This is a local overlap measurement, not a live-game speedup benchmark or a separately run serial client. The same run observed six concurrent GETs, one mutation at a time, no GET/mutation overlap, and recovery after a `Retry-After: 1` refusal.

The runner must also wake without events for berth unlocks, cooldowns, deadlines, expected arrivals, and freshness expiry. Synchronous orders and unlocked berths are not guaranteed to produce the events a mission would need to progress.

Private books and shipyard quotes require fresh authoritative stationary ship presence or factor access. A sailing ship's retained origin and a mission's intended origin are not presence. Public route discovery remains separate from private-data authorization.

Successful public route responses are cached by origin, destination and hull for 24 real hours, retaining their original observation timestamps. The [route API](https://tradewinds.kibb.io/api-docs/openapi.json) returns static legs, distances and edge classes plus hull-derived expected/safe provisions and port consumption, not live hazards, prices or balances. Its default `max_phase` is the world's unlocked phase, so a phase change invalidates the cache, including known illegal paths. Refresh fetches only missing, expired or failed entries; cached geometry is usable across observation generations. Responses must match their requested endpoints and any requested hull class. The persisted route group drops unrequested pairs except the bounded illegal sourcing history described below. Books, quotes, vaults, ships, contracts, live hazards and every other executable observation retain current-generation freshness.

Route origins come from authoritative stationary fleet or factor presence, not a planned hire. A newly hired expansion pair therefore needs the next refresh to request its private books, yard quote and outward/return geometry. A refresh already in progress cannot expand its child requests after the hire receipt. The resulting one-pass route-fetch stall is expected; it does not authorize use of historical gossip as executable books.

Clock bounds require a complete successful current-generation capture with valid request/completion ordering and a fresh host timestamp. Failed, future-dated or stale observations cannot authorize new mutations. A new-send preflight checks the same conditions after rate-limit waits; persisted ambiguous or completed actions retain their independent reconciliation path.

### Passenger capture and authority

[Passenger capture](../internal/engine/passenger_capture.go) reads `passenger_offers`, grouped `passenger_waiting` by stationary fleet port, grouped `passenger_aboard` by ship and grouped `passenger_fares` by directed lane. Offers use the open-only paginated endpoint. Aboard reads cover open offers, occupied holds and boarding hints. Fare reads follow observed waiting lanes and are limited to once per 10 game hours per lane, including failed attempts and restarts. Failed captures and brief departures do not reset this gate. Fare data and the gate expire only after 10 authoritative game hours of proven lane irrelevance, with no relevant waiting lane, offer or aboard group. Optional capture failure keeps the last good bytes with stale/error metadata; it does not turn unavailable passengers into an empty success. These endpoints and their ownership rules are defined in the [guide, passengers](https://tradewinds.kibb.io/skill.md).

`passengers_boarded` invalidates ship authority before another mutation. Paid and lost events also invalidate financial authority. Aboard passengers require a matching durable passage before departure. Passage intent retains the cost floor, hourly ladder, offer identity, whole groups and arrival promises. Required provisioning precedes offshore movement and offer posting. A short boarding deadline starts at the floor rather than promising a later floor step. Once groups board, lateness alone does not veto departure. The journal owns offer/cancel retries; an offer-exists conflict resolves from authoritative offers. Transient passenger failures hold and retry with the original intent. Only integrity failures quarantine, persisting `passage.quarantine_reason` and a `passage_quarantined` decision. [Recovery](../internal/engine/passenger_quarantine.go) archives damaged accounting, cancels offers and navigates using fresh aboard evidence without manufacturing fare receipts. Empty-aboard and no-open-offer proof releases the operating slot and closes its archives. Unrelated work continues. See [passenger execution](../internal/engine/passengers.go) and [event fencing](../internal/engine/runner.go).

Ordinary queued events request another capture without indefinitely vetoing a safe mutation after the current capture. Stream gaps, authorization controls, and stream closure remain barriers: their published control epoch prevents writes against an earlier capture, even while a control waits behind ordinary events. Company billing, remittance, tax, seizure and bankruptcy events use a separate financial epoch. Captured spending decisions carry that epoch and the runner instance through staging. A stale never-sent request is replanned after authoritative refresh; an existing journal identity remains authoritative for ambiguous or completed outcomes. A first-attempt guard runs after the transport's rate-limit wait. If it vetoes a newly journaled request before transport, durable explicit no-send evidence permits safe replanning across restart. This guards known local invalidation, not atomicity with upstream cash changes.

Loan quote-received, request-expired, disbursement, repayment, default, collateral seizure, write-off, foreclosure surplus and insurance-to-lender events join that financial fence. They trigger refreshed REST debt, request, fleet and cash authority before financial first sends; event payloads and balance changes alone are never loan receipts.

A proven never-sent precondition, terms or quote veto retires only its owning mission's local action and persists a 15-game-minute retry (or its existing domain retry), yielding the same work pass to other missions and ships. Routine financial/control epoch invalidation, including restart, instead replans or restamps on the next pass without adding a cooldown. Ambiguous or possibly sent journal outcomes retain the account spending barrier. Transient expansion hires persist both reciprocal records' `retry_at`, no later than their original two-hour expiry. Admission and first-send share projected post-transfer funding, including the new regional base and pending fees exactly once. A capacity funding remittance pins its beneficiary mission and generation; first-send excludes only that exact beneficiary's own reservation, as staging does. An existing treasury intent, including its cooldown, cannot be overwritten by another funding generation.

Invalidated never-sent standalone maintenance intents are released so idle planning can reconsider the ship from fresh state. Voyage missions retain their cargo and obligations; ambiguous or completed journal requests are never discarded by this release.

First-send monetary guards recheck fresh financial authority and the stage-specific funded commitment, including ship-less construction. Cash-funded construction reuses the planner's quote, payback, spare depth and commissioning-reserve checks; financed construction uses its frozen admission and live yard-ceiling, regional funding, debt-reserve and coverage checks. Every build using a `zone estimate`, including cash, mortgage and financed builds, also rechecks fresh net worth and the gross estimated-exposure cap. The transport callback checks the captured financial epoch both before and after that work, so a financial event published during preflight invalidates the never-sent request. Missing or failed balance observations cannot authorize a new debit; already attempted requests retain their immutable recovery path.

An estimated-hazard financed admission pins the depth-backed earning destination as well as the hazard source. First-send preflight validates that exact route against current geometry, storm season and current piracy with the calibrated leg-length and arrival-risk limits; missing, negative or out-of-bound target risk refuses the build even when the frozen financial package is still sound.

The published stream-control epoch also fences staging, preflight and the actual first send after a rate-limit wait. A gap or closure invalidates an earlier capture even if its balances still satisfy their age limit. Only an intent proven never sent may be released and replanned; attempted requests retain the exact durable identity and payload through reconciliation.

## Rate limits and retry ownership

The transport owns individual attempts, bounded transient retries, and rate-limit waits. The engine owns mission recovery, SSE reconnection, resynchronization, and economic replanning. Both use cancellation.

A process-wide public release watcher runs independently of account runners and trading, including when no account can trade. Its unauthenticated client reads the configured API's `/update` at startup and every 15 real minutes, with limiter feedback isolated from authenticated clients and a public cooldown capped at one hour. The first successful feed establishes the baseline even when empty; the first later release warns. Restarts establish a new informational baseline. A feed omitting the last-seen release is ignored without advancing its cursor. Polls emit at most five release warnings with bounded fields and headings extracted outside fenced code. Consecutive failures log once per streak and use jittered exponential retries starting within one minute and capped at one hour. This watcher neither blocks trading nor authorizes investment mutations.

| Bucket | Per-key burst / refill per second | Shared-IP allowance |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Read | 2000 / 100 | 5 times key allowance |
| Write | 1000 / 50 | 5 times key allowance |
| Batch | 300 / 10 | 5 times key allowance |
| Stream | 200 / 10 | 5 times key allowance |
| Authentication | Not key-based | 20 / 0.2 |

Authentication, stream routes, and batch routes must be classified before the generic HTTP method. Every retry consumes applicable tokens. Apply cooldown to the affected bucket rather than blocking all reads and all accounts unnecessarily. Respect `Retry-After`; a cached mutation 429 is not a reason to rotate an ambiguous action key.

SSE scanner errors preserve their original cause. Only `bufio.ErrTooLong` is reported as the 1 MiB line limit; interrupted chunks and connection failures are transport errors handled by the existing reconnect policy.

These shared-IP limiters coordinate one process. Separate hosts or processes behind the same public IP still share the upstream allowance; PostgreSQL ownership protects a company, not the entire network's request budget.

## Persistence lifetime

Goose migration [00022_bulletin](../internal/store/migrations/00022_bulletin.sql) adds global `bulletin_threads`, `bulletin_posts` and singleton `bulletin_sync` records, independent of account lifetime and the seven-day diagnostic prune. Account ownership still fences every mirror write. Listing updates preserve detailed posts and detail freshness, ignore older activity observations, and can replace rows seen only as event stubs. SSE inserts idempotent stubs without replacing authoritative text; only a newly recorded post invalidates detail, so replays across accounts do not trigger duplicate refreshes. Full thread reads replace edits, removed/deleted text and reply counts in place: this mirrors the game's current public text, not revision history. Titles, company names and bodies are bounded at 300, 120 and 8,000 Unicode characters, with unsafe controls stripped except newline and tab. Links must be bounded JSON objects smaller than 1 KiB; NUL, invalid UTF-8 and unpaired surrogate escapes produce a null link rather than rejecting the page.

Goose migration [00021_snapshot_routes](../internal/store/migrations/00021_snapshot_routes.sql) moves the `routes` payload out of `snapshots.data` into `snapshot_routes`, keyed by account and route key. Route freshness metadata, including `_meta` entries for `routes` and `routes/<key>`, remains in the 16 MiB account snapshot row alongside cache invalidation state. The lease-fenced save commits that row and route inserts, updates and deletions in one transaction; unchanged route payloads do not rewrite route rows. `Store.Snapshot` reads a coherent merged snapshot in a read-only repeatable-read transaction for runner recovery.

The route payload has an independent 64 MiB per-account limit on the canonical Go-encoded route object, including route keys, JSON escaping and numeric expansion: each number is budgeted at up to 64 rendered characters, a conservative estimate that covers exponent forms. The bound is checked before and after credential redaction. Exceeding it rejects the save with `ErrResponseTooLarge`; it is not an unbounded cache or an automatic eviction policy. The 16 MiB snapshot row limit is unchanged and separate: non-route data and route metadata must still fit it.

Stored route reads preflight the stored size before hydration, so an oversized stored payload cannot expand unbounded in memory. A snapshot row that still embeds routes while schema 21 route rows exist fails closed instead of merging ambiguous state. An invalid route capture preserves the last good entry and marks stale/error metadata rather than persisting the invalid response. Optional route history retains its existing 256-route and 1 MiB caps including metadata, with at most 1,024 candidates scanned; it is no longer evicted to make shared snapshot headroom. Replay has a separate 16 MiB aggregate plaintext frame limit: startup and sweep captures can omit the largest datasets, including routes, as explicit metadata-only gaps even when operational route storage succeeds.

Raw request/response diagnostics, event payloads, and decision traces have a seven-day retention window. Passenger events at or after an account's earliest open receipt start are protected until those receipts resolve or close; the receipt's creation time is the fallback if its settlement is not yet present. Active passage missions also protect events since their start so pruning cannot remove terminal proof between an engine read and settlement. Unrelated accounts, earlier passenger events and non-passenger diagnostics keep the normal window. Current snapshots, encrypted credentials, mission recovery state, unresolved actions, idempotent settlement records, and metrics survive that window. Settlement event identities and passenger receipt ownership links remain durable independently of raw event payloads. Action journals are operational recovery records, not disposable request logs.

Passenger recovery reads use those same account-scoped windows rather than hiding protected events behind the ordinary seven-day cutoff. Mission rows are scanned in bounded composite-key batches before receipt windows are read, so the receipt-before-mission-release handoff cannot create a protection gap. Unreadable mission rows conservatively protect that account's passenger events and emit one aggregate warning without stopping other diagnostic pruning.

Goose version 9 adds the replay tape, separate from capped seven-day diagnostics. `replay_payloads` stores redacted dataset bytes once per account using streamed, purpose-separated HMAC-SHA256 digests, gzip and AES-GCM; unaffected bytes stay exact. Events use a separate digest domain and uncompressed encrypted storage to avoid a mixed-trust compression oracle. Aggregate frame plaintext and decompression are bounded at 16 MiB, with a 4 MiB gzip cap. Frame preparation bounds raw evidence before parsing and bounds the redacted representation again, so credential replacement or JSON escaping cannot create a permanent oversize failure. Largest datasets become reasoned metadata-only gaps; metadata/name-only overflow becomes a compact frame gap instead of stopping either recorder. Live losses increment the process-local recorder error counter and warn once per account/cause. Encrypted compressed `replay_frames` manifests bind dataset keys and `DatasetMeta` to those digests. Payload locks and last-seen updates share the frame transaction; sorted digests and batched inserts avoid random lock ordering and ciphertext round trips. Manifest AAD binds a random portable capture identity and all semantic headers, not bigserial IDs, allowing authorized transfers between databases without unauthenticated clock, source or timestamp edits. Only validated public clock scalars are plaintext; fields with unexpected types are omitted independently while raw clock evidence remains encrypted. `ScanReplayFrames` keyset-pages by `(observed_at, id)` and retains only the preceding frame's decrypted digest cache.

`replay_plans` is append-only accepted plan and policy evidence, with ship, phase, revision and plan context encrypted together and generation, sequence and observation time authenticated. The recorder deduplicates the same semantic private tuple as the store. `replay_action_links` joins persisted mission/action identities without copying the authoritative mutation journal. Both survive tape pruning; account deletion cascades through all four tables. Replay writes use `Store.query`, never executor ownership guards, so a timeout cannot cancel the live lease.

`tw-sim accuracy [--account <label-or-ID>] [--from RFC3339] [--to RFC3339] [--fit-until RFC3339] [--out accuracy.json]` measures recorded admission forecasts against realized settlements without game requests, migrations or database writes. It holds the same repeatable-read snapshot as `validate` and `run`, bounds plan reads to missions settled in the selected window and authenticates their encrypted context. Admission uses the earliest plan observed no later than one second after mission start; latest-before-completion forecasts are a separate table, never replacements for admission. Unknown costs/proceeds, missing plans, already-sailing first captures, quantity mismatches and unpriced passage forecasts remain explicit coverage. Fit and holdout are split by the specified boundary, defaulting to the window midpoint. Bad forecasts do not fail the command; only incomplete evidence budgets produce exit 2. `--out` preserves deterministic JSON for comparisons.

Accuracy uses `LoadAccuracyTape` to read only the recorded calibration boundaries from the operational snapshot and load `Tape.Plans` through `ReplayPlansForMissions`, with 100-row metadata pages, authenticated single-row reads, a 10,000-row cap and a separate 32 MiB retained-plan budget. It skips unrelated action/accounting evidence; `run` and `validate` retain their original loaders and budgets without a new dependency on plans. Bounded settlement projections include encrypted admission forecasts and nullable terminal forecast, basis and shortfall fields. The JSON separates old point, old stressed, new median and new stressed scores on the same full-lot holdout, accepted subsets and rejected realized totals. The required durable-quote revenue split has a cost/reprice bridge, not a pure cost error; additional selected-plan revenue and cost misses remove that repricing bridge. This is fixed-lot forecast/admission comparison, not replacement-voyage simulation.

Voyage creation freezes uncalibrated admission revenue, exact horizon, basis, quantity and event-stress flag in encrypted mission context, then copies it into settlement context. Production fits with one settlement query and no per-minute replay joins. The rival collector discovers IDs from saved evidence, monopolies, futures, banks and loan requests. The automatic futures controller uses `__futures` and the shared immutable journal, without an admission environment switch, one permanent experiment or operator re-arm path. Mature obligations and recovery precede new commitments. The controller assesses warehouse-covered sales into player bids and atomic purchase-before-sale crosses. Own quotes, downstream ship purchases and the ship-cargo handoff are deleted. Fixed Amsterdam/`water_beer`, one-ton admissions and 85%/115% midpoint crossing prices are retired. Accessible hub presence and fresh books, stock, cash and server clock authority remain required, together with a fresh player profile excluding all our player's companies and usable counterparty credit evidence.

Standalone seller coverage is unlocked owned warehouse stock net of active sale reservations and terminal surplus awaiting liquidation. Crosses preserve lot, commodity, hub and distinct counterparties. Their purchase must mature at least one game hour before their sale and delivers into leased warehouse capacity; no opening stock is required. Purchases within a cross reserve receiving space, full strike cash, customs and margin. Both counterparties' credit allowances, both margins' carry and rent reduce estimated net. Mature contracts are serviced in maturity order so a resumed pass delivers the earlier purchase before its sale.

Receiving capacity is port-wide across commodities. Immutable admissions retain modeled rent and journal lease changes before acceptance; lease ownership preserves the prior baseline and releases unused added capacity. Lost capacity or a drifted target rejects a proven-unsent group immediately rather than leaving the first intent to block the controller. Attempted lease mutations retain their immutable request and key.

Futures no longer intercept ship work or journal docking, offshore or cargo transfers. One shared net-stock calculation includes both purchase and sale delivery receipts and prior liquidation. Terminal surplus purchases, including old downstream buys without an owning mission, exit through journaled one-ton IOC sales at the NPC bid. Per-cohort exit quantity, proceeds and unknown-cash evidence persist with mission clearing; physical stock and pending receiving obligations protect the lease until released.

Admission uses at most 10% of free regional cash after commitments, a 25% book concentration limit per counterparty and a 2,000,000-pence final sanity bound. Estimated improvement over the executable spot alternative must be at least the larger of 100 pence or 5%, after customs, rent allowance, margin carry and credit stress. A usable report carries at least 5% notional credit stress, rising to 25% for historical defaults or claims; stale/missing reports and open claims cannot authorize new work. These estimates are explicit admission evidence, not calibrated default probabilities or simulated futures P&L. The local simulator has no futures model.

Cheap side, lot, port, commodity and counterparty checks precede pair valuation; terminal history cannot multiply ship-mission database reads.

Futures and exchange SSE events invalidate list authority; public listings never fence unrelated fleet cash, while own cash-changing events retain the financial epoch. Successful settled stream evidence may omit default-only penalty/claim fields, which normalize to zero. Legacy Amsterdam stock and lease wind-down runs even when no new commitment qualifies. `futures_done` requires the lease to return to `InitialLease` or below and excess owned stock to be accounted for; terminal contract state alone is insufficient. A legacy exit wait does not disable unrelated opportunities or release its remaining stock for a new sale. Transient never-sent preconditions retain admissions and persist a cooldown while settlement and fleet work continue. Immutable-term or reserve violations stop the affected admission. Positive no-send proof releases unsent reservations. An ambiguous accept or atomic batch can also be rejected from matching open or other-party listings fetched after a persisted observation fence: the later of reconciliation time or the last transport attempt's completion plus the 45-second request timeout and a five-second margin. Missing attempt timing conservatively starts that wait at reconciliation. Any negative batch leg proves the entire atomic request did not commit at observation time. Journaled negative proof survives a crash before mission clearing, but a later exact listing naming us as a party re-adopts even a rejected intent with its original reservations. Final settlement receipts cannot be resurrected. Stock reconciliation uses the original inventory baseline and unique private fills without inventing cash.

Recorded cohort P&L requires measured contract cash and complete physical-stock cost basis, including legacy procurement and liquidation receipts where applicable. Unpriced stock, inventory-only trade cash and unmeasured rent remain unknown; margin returns and principal are not profit. Default liability clears only when validated post-default own credit reports zero open claims. Matching penalty cash can attribute an unpaid claim but cannot discharge it. Last validated positive aggregate claims stay reserved until a later valid reduction, replacing only older per-contract estimates, with conservative coverage for newer defaults. Own-credit attempts are durable and limited to once per five real minutes while unresolved claims remain; failures preserve the last valid amount and do not enter rival attempt, report or identity tables.

`underworld-key` stores a separate AES-GCM-encrypted company key with `[read, underworld]` scopes. The main key stays unchanged. ### Engine warnings and build identity

`Store.Warnings(ctx, at)` projects recorded fields for enabled, registered accounts in a read-only transaction: engine and dataset metadata, ships, clock, ports, grouped details, bounded arrival/departure evidence, latest known-cost commercial settlements, published rising growth and the 24-hour fallback benchmark. `AssessWarnings` applies fixed thresholds and display scrubbing without game calls or mutation. Critical warnings sort before warnings, then by code and subject. [Engine warnings](runbook.md#engine-warnings) gives thresholds and actions.

The nine codes are `controller_stalled` (critical, completed controller observation at least 10 real minutes old), `refresh_failing` (critical, a required ships/vaults/contracts/clock update at least 10 minutes old with a current controller), `snapshot_near_limit` (warning when stored JSON text bytes reach 50% of `store.MaxSnapshotBytes`, critical at 80% or while any dataset metadata records `store.SnapshotShedError`; independent of fleet assessment), `loan_payments_at_risk` (warning when vault cash covers less than one real day of installments on own active amortised unsecured non-mortgage loans, critical below two installments or with any arrears; suppressed unless loans, vaults and clock are current and the clock runs), `upkeep_seizure` (critical, a gone hull seized by the bailiff or after bankruptcy because upkeep went unpaid), `fleet_idle` (at least half of known active ships, minimum one, docked and free at least 30 minutes), `hold_stuck` (longest qualifying ship hold after at least 90 minutes free), `loss_streak` (latest three known-cost commercial completions negative), and `falling_behind`. Snapshot byte facts measure PostgreSQL's rendered JSON text, not the compact encoding limited at save time: warnings can fire early, and rendered bytes can slightly exceed the compact limit. Shed dataset names collapse child keys to their parent dataset. The last warning uses published rising growth when at least three players are ranked, otherwise negative 24-hour valuation change below at least three measured rivals. The rising window is the last seven real days, or shorter when a player's first hourly snapshot is later. Both need a publication no older than two hours. Commercial kinds are `spot`, `charter`, `freight`, `spot+freight`, `charter+freight`, `recovery` and `aborted`; probes and unknown costs are skipped.

Confirmed upkeep seizures remain historical warnings while their hulls are retained, even when refresh fails; they are not claims that current vault funds are still insufficient. Without a complete, successful, non-stale fleet observation less than two real minutes old, the absence of a seizure warning is unassessed.

Idle/hold assessment requires complete, error-free, non-stale ships and engine snapshots with `updated_at` less than two real minutes old, an unpaused clock and non-observe mode. An all-unknown fleet (`known=0`, `unknown>0`) is unassessed. Free time starts after the later docking and turnaround-unlock timestamps, using `berthed_at` or `dock_queued_at`, then a newer grouped `data.ship_details[id]` observation if absent; its metadata key remains `ship_details/<id>`. The final fallback is the latest recorded `ship_arrived.created_at`, only after the latest departure and while fresh state still says docked/no voyage. Its `names.since_source` is `arrival_event`, and presentation labels this as since recorded arrival (receipt time). Missing time stays unknown and is excluded from the known fleet denominator, never treated as idle.

The arrival fallback selects only docked/no-voyage ship IDs that still lack docking-clock evidence after the shared grouped-detail preference. Its newest-first arrival/departure query is bounded to seven days and 10,000 rows. Transactional Goose migration `00011_warning_arrivals.sql` adds `events_warning_arrivals_idx ON events (account_id, created_at DESC, id DESC) WHERE event_type IN ('ship_arrived', 'ship_departed')`; the query uses this exact predicate so unrelated account events are excluded from the index scan. Up/down use `IF NOT EXISTS`/`IF EXISTS`, leave event rows untouched and commit atomically like version 10. A truncated history can leave a ship unknown but cannot hide a departure newer than a retained arrival.

`GET /api/warnings` returns `generated_at`, real `build {revision, modified, started_at}`, `accounts [{account_id, label, available, error_code, suppressed, warnings}]` and `unmonitored [{account_id, reason}]`. Disabled, unregistered and empty-API-key registrations are explicitly excluded with reasons `disabled`, `unregistered` and `no_api_key`. Account-local corrupt credentials, malformed snapshots or oversized snapshots leave healthy accounts available in HTTP 200 and use only safe `error_code` values `credentials_unavailable`, `snapshot_invalid` or `snapshot_too_large`; corrupt credentials remain monitored, not unregistered. Each warning carries `code`, `severity`, `since`, integer `facts`, scrubbed `names` and `reason`. Revision comes from `runtime/debug.ReadBuildInfo`'s `vcs.revision`, or is null; modified is the actual VCS flag and started_at is process start, never invented from a requested filename. Nil storage or a failed database read returns 503 `database_unavailable`, not a successful all-clear. The startup `dashboard API listening` INFO line includes the same revision/modified identity.

`suppressed` means evidence was not assessable, not healthy. Missing, zero or future engine time suppresses controller and refresh; a valid stalled controller suppresses refresh. Missing required update times suppress refresh when no valid stale timestamp qualifies. Stale, incomplete or errored fleet/engine data, pause, observe mode and an all-unknown fleet suppress idle/hold. With at least three ranked rising players, stale publication, missing own window or no non-owned entries suppresses `falling_behind` without fallback. Otherwise missing or stale valuation endpoints, our change, median or three measured rivals suppresses it. Fewer than three known commercial completions is an assessed negative for loss-streak.

The process-wide watcher is independent of account runners, evaluates immediately and each real minute, raises WARN `engine warning` once per `(account_id, code)`, repeats WARN `engine warning persists` immediately when severity changes or when an assessed active key has not been logged for 30 real minutes, and clears once at INFO with `active_for` only after an available, unsuppressed negative assessment. Changing facts alone does not re-raise an active key. Missing accounts, account-local failures and suppressed codes retain prior state without false clear/raise transitions. Global read failures emit WARN `engine warnings unavailable` once per streak with safe `error` (`database_unavailable` or `context_deadline_exceeded`); account-local unavailable streaks include account identity and safe `error` derived from the account error code. Cancellation is silent. Restart establishes a new in-memory baseline and raises measured active warnings once. Logs retain transitions, not a new warning-history table.

The browser shares one global 30-second warnings query and renders only its current report. Successful responses do not retain earlier warning lines for missing/unavailable accounts or suppressed codes; that retention belongs to the log watcher, not the UI. Actual warning lines and genuine unavailability for monitored accounts appear on every view. Explicitly unmonitored accounts, including empty-API-key registrations, get only an Overview not-monitored note rather than a missing-evaluation error. Suppression/not-assessed notes, quiet all-clear and build context are Overview-only; a nonempty fully assessed monitored scope is required for all-clear. Other views render nothing without actual warnings or genuine monitored-account unavailability. A request failure alone keeps the cached last successful response with a could-not-update note, or says `Engine warnings unavailable` for monitored scope if no response was cached. No warnings is not proof of profit.

[Fast restart](runbook.md#fast-restart) stages builds, preflights binary VCS metadata and limits shutdown to executable paths under the delivery directory. Default deployments reject tracked Go changes, then build the captured HEAD revision in a temporary detached Git worktree with checkout hooks disabled. Cleanup runs in `finally` with double-force removal, one retry and registration pruning; cleanup diagnostics preserve an earlier build error. Builds disable workspaces, set the nonempty `GOFLAGS=-mod=readonly` to override saved flags without discarding other persisted Go settings, and use `-mod=readonly`. Native Git/Go calls check exit codes with nonterminating error handling for Windows PowerShell 5.1 redirected-stderr compatibility. Automatic reuse requires the full HEAD revision and `vcs.modified=false`; otherwise the script rebuilds. `-AllowDirty` always rebuilds the working tree under a `<hash>-dirty` name. Explicit `-Version` rollback may select a modified or metadata-incomplete artifact with a printed warning, but a present revision must match its requested prefix. The Ctrl+C helper checks exclusive console ownership before broadcasting, otherwise waits for the selected-PID forced fallback. Explorer owns the replacement console. Normal success requires readiness, the new executable process path and matching API build identity; explicit legacy rollback can report `running, revision unverifiable` after readiness/path confirmation. This is an operator command, not a crash supervisor.

Verified replacements are installed before shutdown unless the target path is the running image. Same-version replacements then launch from their retained, process-selectable staged filename. A later restart promotes the newest verified staged artifact to its canonical versioned filename once neither path is running, without replacing a newer verified canonical build; stale nonrunning staged files are pruned while running images and the latest pending replacement are preserved. Once shutdown begins, failure handling preserves the verified executable and prints the exact manual relaunch command rather than deleting the replacement. Printed command arguments use single-quoted PowerShell literals with doubled apostrophes, preventing directory names from becoming expressions when pasted.

### Recorded dashboard views

The global Bulletin view reads `/api/bulletin?q=&company=&limit=` and `/api/bulletin/thread?id=<uuid>` from PostgreSQL only. The board defaults to 200 threads (maximum 500), ordered by newest activity then ID; search is a literal case-insensitive substring over title, company and post body, and the exact company filter includes every poster. Board responses include matching counts, at most 1,000 nonempty company names with `companies_truncated`, and the last completed head scan's start time. Thread responses include a newest-post window of at most 1,000 recorded posts and 8 MiB of encoded JSON (including escaping), displayed oldest first, with honest `posts_total` and `posts_truncated`, per-post edit/removal/deletion states, detail sync time and `detail_current` coverage. The reader streams rows newest first and stops at the byte budget rather than hydrating the entire history. Archive status reflects 72 real hours since the newer of listed activity and the newest recorded post. Both use read-only repeatable-read transactions, never game requests. The dashboard polls recorded data every 30 seconds while visible and preserves the selected thread.

The mirror consumes `bulletin_posted` SSE without a game request. Shared diagnostic persistence replaces decoded NUL and malformed UTF-16 in JSON strings and keys with U+FFFD, retaining safe content and allowing the event cursor to advance. At the idle tail of fleet work, independently of execution and posting toggles, it atomically claims a global board-list attempt at most once per ten real minutes across runners and restarts. Head scans use a stable `since` watermark with a two-minute overlap and retain a cursor until exhausted; head freshness advances only on completion. The first head scan starts two real minutes before collection. Remaining page capacity serves the independent full-history backfill cursor, with at most five 100-thread pages total per claim. Saved cursors reset on HTTP 400/404/410/422 or an invalid page, not on transient 408/429 responses; malformed listing rows are skipped. Up to two detail IDs are atomically claimed with row locks and `SKIP LOCKED`; unrequested claims are released without backoff, and a 404 marks detail synced without inventing a removal. Detail posts are saved with set-based upserts of at most 1,000 rows. Definitive authorization refusals stop execution immediately, including during detail loops. Reads and writes share a three-second pass deadline, with reads stopping a second earlier to reserve write time; releasing unrequested claims has a separate one-second cleanup limit. Collection is skipped within six seconds of the next wake or trading deadline, including the optional posting budget. Failures record one `__bulletin_mirror` decision when budget remains; exhausted budgets log without issuing a late diagnostic write. Parent cancellation, ownership loss and execution stops still propagate. SSE mirror storage failures are logged without ending the event loop.

Detail freshness records the observed reply count and each post's edit deadline. New activity or invalidation takes priority over never-attempted backfill and periodic refresh. Attempts back off from ten real minutes, doubling to six hours, unless new activity invalidates detail after that attempt. A post's edit window closing after the last fetch, or six real hours without detail for an active, non-removed thread, makes detail stale; derived archived threads do not enter periodic refresh. Database attempt time dates successful detail observations, avoiding host-clock skew. This catches edits and removals even when no new post advances activity time.

The World view shows one calm region list with native [disclosures](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/details) for ports. Migration [00025_company_ledger_flows](../internal/store/migrations/00025_company_ledger_flows.sql) retains account-scoped ledger receipts permanently. Owned-to-external entries are negative and external-to-owned entries positive. The single money plot has a metric switcher and persisted 24h/7d/30d/All presets plus custom UTC date-and-time controls. Custom inputs remain local drafts until Apply range commits valid bounds; historical custom ranges do not poll. The interaction follows [financial range-selector guidance](https://www.highcharts.com/docs/stock/range-selector): quick presets plus precise date inputs. A second overview/brush is deliberately omitted to keep touch and keyboard operation simple and avoid another chart. Gain/loss bars use shared theme tokens, signed values and above/below-zero position; the keyboard point inspector and value table provide [non-color and textual alternatives](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/). The vertical axis covers the recorded range and zero using round 1/2/5 steps in whole Crowns; exact pence remain in the inspector and table. Loading and failed retrieval are distinct from missing observations. Balance freshness warnings apply only to ranges ending near the response's generation time, not deliberate historical views. Background refresh and pointer inspection do not repeatedly announce changing values. The existing Plex typography and nautical neutral surfaces stay quiet around the working chart.

World > Standings reads `/api/standings-history?account_id=…&range=24h|7d|30d|all&top=5`; `top` accepts 1 to 20. It uses inclusive official valuation times and the same preset control, monetary axes and chart styling as Money. The chart defaults to our latest recorded company and the directly adjacent ranks from that same official snapshot, requesting the available top 20 to find those rivals. Top 5/10/20 plus our player remain selectable. Accessible legend toggles hide individual lines and rescale the axis to visible series. Repeatable-read summaries calculate exact first/last integer-pence changes before downsampling. The API deliberately retains its exact first/last values, observation count and change as range summaries, distinct from Rivals' fixed 24-hour comparison. Each charted series has at most 481 last-per-UTC-bucket observations plus its exact first observation; missing intervals remain missing. A single observation has null change, never an invented zero. Our player has a heavier solid line, and the full official standings and valuation-method disclosures remain available below the chart.

The [official leaderboard contract](https://tradewinds.kibb.io/api-docs/openapi.json) recomputes every real hour and publishes best-company names, home ports, integer net worth and titles, but **no player handles or IDs**. The runner fetches it with each full reconciliation (a normal idle wake is at most 15 real seconds, with event/action-driven wakes sooner). Historical series therefore remain separate by season and published best-company name: no guessed joins across renamed/replaced companies or seasons, and no reconstructed valuations before retained evidence. Source refresh/next-refresh times determine the explicit stale state, not browser fetch time.

The Rivals block adds a two-row Growth vs rivals table ahead of three qualified hunch lines and one searchable comparison table. `/api/rivals?account_id=…` reads saved evidence in one read-only repeatable-read transaction and never calls the game or scans request diagnostics. Hunch change compares the latest official publication with the nearest publication in the same season within 60 minutes of 24 real hours earlier. Missing names or publications leave change null; a name first valued less than 24 hours earlier is too new, otherwise it is unmeasured. Identity is the exact `(season_number, company_name)` pair, with no fuzzy matches or inherited values between similarly named companies. Known configured company names and the account's `standings_accounts` name are excluded. When own names are incomplete, the observed list may include our other companies, so all three verdicts are withheld with reason `own_companies_incomplete`. Companies remain listed when any measured fact is retained: the latest valuation, rising rank, explicit ID-to-company-name observation, seven-day ship sighting, or expansion contribution. A name alone does not establish valuation, activity, or profit. Bank names are not treated as company names.

`RivalReport.growth` returns 6-hour then 24-hour windows. `growthBenchmark` matches the baseline nearest `t1-window` within plus or minus 60 minutes in the same season. Matched `from` and `to` are source times or null. Our `standings_accounts` identity must exist at both endpoints with the same best-company name. Rival gaps stay unmeasured. An even median averages the middle integer-pence changes with truncation toward zero. These are valuation changes, not realized profit. Six hours is UI-only; 24 hours is the warning fallback. With at least three ranked rising players, compare our published rising growth bps (up to seven real days) strictly below the median non-owned entry, including positive own growth. Warning `names.basis` distinguishes `rising` from `valuation_24h`; unusable rising evidence at that size suppresses assessment instead of changing basis.

The rising board exposes its own `rising_refreshed_at` publication time and `rising_stale` flag, independently of official valuation freshness. A missing, future, or more-than-two-real-hour-old publication is stale. The dashboard explicitly shows missing fields from an older API as not observed; journal rows without IDs stay separate and disclose the missing identity. Half-basis-point rival medians retain their fractional remainder in `median_growth_half_bps` instead of displaying an apparent tie. Credit candidate discovery and collection run at most once per runner per real hour, ordered by published rank then least recent attempt. One durable batch reserves only the remaining shared hourly budget, excludes configured companies and six-hour cooldowns, and never refunds uncertain sends. Collection waits for a known publication season so a lookup can be retained. Optional collection failures warn and never halt trading.
Seven-day observed ship counts share one bounded event scan with 24-hour traffic. `observed_ships_7d_truncated` independently labels partial seven-day counts as lower bounds; old sightings cannot mark an otherwise complete 24-hour traffic window truncated.

Hunch precedence is `most_dangerous > plays_rough > threat > losing_value > growing > steady > too_new > unmeasured > not_ranked`. Most dangerous is the largest rival above our same-publication net worth with at least one shared lane or port; only unknown overlap permits the largest-above-us fallback. Threat requires at least our net worth and observed overlap. Neither applies when our valuation is missing. A positive measured change is growing and zero is steady, even if net worth remains negative; the negative-worth fact preserves that distinction. Otherwise negative net worth or a negative measured change is losing value. Rows follow hunch precedence, then net worth descending with missing values last, then exact name. The three verdicts are selected independently of the row's one hunch and fact.

Plays rough uses attributable evidence, strongest first: an in-force, unexpired monopoly from a complete, error-free snapshot updated within 30 real minutes; the count of recorded monopoly grants in seven days; the latest recorded borrower record's positive walk-away count; then its positive default count. Missing or zero credit records and stale or expired monopolies do not establish rough play or a clean record. Evidence only for companies outside the listed rivals yields `unlisted_only`, not a claim that no rough play was recorded. The headline selects the strongest listed evidence; if Most dangerous takes that company's row chip, the caption explains the precedence. The not-profitable hunch line selects the most negative net worth, otherwise the largest relative 24-hour decline with a positive baseline, otherwise no company. That label is an inference from valuation, not measured rival profit. Float64 is used only to rank relative declines; monetary values remain integer pence.

Traffic combines bounded sailing events and quay notices. Lanes are unordered port pairs; shared ports need a direct `port_id` sighting. Missing own identities or notices leaves overlap unknown, not zero. Reads disclose truncation. Migration 10 adds the rival-event partial index transactionally; deploys stop the old bot first. Index duration and lock time are not guaranteed. Migration 17 separately adds permanent credit and rising evidence. Per-class observed ship identities span 24 real hours and seven real days; expansion contributions explain valuation, not recurring revenue. Search, sorting and pagination retain state across refreshes. Piracy sponsors, attacks, blockades, dumping and rival profit are not inferred as facts; public marque sponsors are anonymous.

The company Journal merges recorded company events, whitelisted successful action outcomes, and durable settlements in one bounded, repeatable-read history query. Company scope is applied before the limit. The store selects lightweight action metadata first, then loads and projects one encrypted outcome at a time. Combined request/response ciphertext above 128 KiB is not loaded into this view; the entry honestly reports unavailable details while the original durable bytes remain intact. Lazy credential reads use the same transaction connection, so concurrent Journal reads never each wait for a second pooled connection. Raw request/response payloads are never returned. Transfers and collateral are labeled separately from revenue, and unknown-cost settlements never become zero-cost profit. A recorded action and its later settlement describe different facts, not two amounts to add together.

The interface separates fleet operations, money, trade, world conditions, and system diagnostics. Neutral charcoal and light surfaces share the same controls. The chart uses vendored Natural Earth 1:50m land, explicit port coordinates, and clearly labeled estimated ship positions. World conditions initially show active notices and fleet-relevant zones; visible scope controls retain access to the complete recorded set.

Overview shows account/company status, each ship's observed location and cargo, current blocker, and the controller's next recorded check directly. `_engine.holds`, `observed_at` and `next_check_at` describe a completed execution cycle in the matching snapshot generation; a fresh dashboard HTTP response alone is not executor liveness. A missing hold assessment is distinct from a completed assessment with no hold. Journal is chronological rather than a nested decision dump, with time, vessel, activity and cash visible together at the 640px layout. Initial map labels prioritize selected and occupied ports before nearby hubs and suppress overlaps. Money directly exposes owned underwriting collateral, premium entitlement, settled net and pilot allowance, including unverified funding holds; Exchange retains public policy listings.

Treasury, fleet-growth and underwriting control holds are company notices, not ships. Their current display attribution has no ship ID; durable decision records keep the internal control identity for diagnostics. Holds for actual ships retain their ship ID even when the corresponding fleet observation is unavailable.

Assessment coverage is explicit: `assessed_ship_ids` lists only ships actually visited in the matching completed generation. Work may return after one staged or consumed action; unvisited ships remain unassessed, not “no hold.” Current sourcing-budget and affordability blockers are attributed to their ship, while treasury, construction and underwriting blockers remain company notices.

## Readability

Source is written for maintenance, not compression. Use idiomatic multi-line Go, descriptive intermediate values, explicit error handling, and focused functions. Gofmt is mandatory but does not make semicolon-packed control flow acceptable. The same principle applies to TypeScript and migration SQL.
